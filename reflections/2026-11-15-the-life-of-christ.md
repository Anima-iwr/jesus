# The Life of Christ

*Discovering Our Identity in Him*

**Scripture for meditation:** John 1:4 · John 14:6 · Galatians 2:20 · 2 Corinthians 5:17 · Colossians 3:3–4 · 1 John 4:17

## More than a better version of ourselves

Many of us spend years asking, *Who am I? What am I capable of? What makes my life valuable?* Our answers can become attached to achievements, mistakes, relationships, or the opinions of others. The Christian gospel introduces a different center: Jesus Christ, who calls people not merely to improve their image, but to receive life in Him.

Jesus says in John 14:6 that He is the way, the truth, and the life. John 1:4 connects life in Him with light for humanity. To know Christ, then, is not simply to collect information about a historical teacher. It is to encounter the One whom the New Testament presents as the source of life and light.

## What does it mean to be “in Christ”?

Second Corinthians 5:17 describes the person in Christ as a new creation. This does not erase personality, memory, or responsibility. It announces a new belonging and a new beginning, grounded in God's grace rather than in a flawless personal record.

Paul writes in Galatians 2:20 about Christ living in him. This language is both intimate and demanding: faith is no longer merely something we discuss; it becomes a way of living, loving, choosing, and serving.

Being united with Christ is not a claim that we become God or possess limitless power independently. It is a call to live by faith, depend on His grace, and grow in likeness to Him.

## From information to participation

There is a difference between knowing *about* generosity and becoming generous; between reading about forgiveness and learning to forgive. In the same way, biblical knowledge is meant to shape our relationships, motives, and actions.

One meditation I return to is this: **the life of Christ brings light; that light changes what we can recognize; what we recognize can change how we live.**

The more we contemplate Jesus in the Gospels—His compassion for the overlooked, His courage before opposition, His truthfulness, His surrender to the Father—the more we have a living pattern for discipleship.

## From glory to glory

Second Corinthians 3:18 describes believers being transformed into the Lord's image. This is a lifelong journey. We may still wrestle with fear, uncertainty, or old habits, but those struggles need not be the final definition of who we are.

Christian identity is not a denial of difficulty. It is a new foundation from which to face difficulty: loved by God, called to follow Christ, and invited to mature in His character.

What would it look like to enter a classroom, laboratory, workplace, or home with that identity? Perhaps it means working diligently without making achievement our worth; speaking confidently without losing humility; offering kindness without demanding recognition; or serving others even when no one is watching.

## Practice this week

1. Read John 14:6 and Galatians 2:20 thoughtfully.
2. Write down three labels you have used to define yourself.
3. Consider which of those labels might need to be placed beneath your identity in Christ.
4. Choose one action that expresses Jesus' character in an ordinary interaction.

## Questions for reflection

- Where do I look for my deepest sense of worth?
- What part of Jesus' character most challenges and attracts me?
- How might my decisions change if I remembered that following Christ is a way of life, not merely a set of beliefs?

**A prayer:** Lord Jesus, let my understanding of who You are deepen. Teach me to find my identity in Your grace, and shape my words, motives, and actions so that others may see Your love through my life. Amen.
